# Legal, data protection and safeguarding compliance for selling a club app to grassroots (incl. youth) football clubs in England

Research date: 19 November 2026. Method note: direct page fetches of ico.org.uk and thefa.com were blocked in this research environment (host not allowed), so ICO and FA material below comes from search-result extracts of those official pages plus law-firm and County FA mirrors. Exact FA wording should be checked against the live PDFs before it is quoted to customers. Nothing here is legal advice.

## 1. UK GDPR roles and the data processing agreement (Article 28)

### Takeaway
For the club's own records (player names, stats, fines, squads), the club is the controller and the app seller acts as its processor, so a written Article 28 contract (DPA) is mandatory. The seller is separately a controller for its own business data (customer billing contacts, and any analytics it runs for its own purposes). Sub-processors (GitHub, OneSignal, any database or payment provider) need the club's prior written authorisation and flow-down contracts.

### Cited Findings
- The ICO lists the minimum terms a controller–processor contract must contain: process only on the controller's documented instructions; duty of confidence on staff; appropriate security measures; rules on using sub-processors; help with data subject rights; help the controller meet its own obligations (security, breach notification, DPIAs); delete or return all data at the end of the contract; provide information and allow audits. Parties may add further terms. — [ICO: What needs to be included in the contract?](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/contracts-and-liabilities-between-controllers-and-processors-multi/what-needs-to-be-included-in-the-contract/); [ICO: When is a contract needed](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/contracts-and-liabilities-between-controllers-and-processors-multi/when-is-a-contract-needed-and-why-is-it-important/)
- Under Article 28(3)(d) / 28(2) the processor needs the controller's prior written authorisation (specific or general) before engaging a sub-processor, must put a contract in place with the sub-processor imposing the same data protection obligations (wording need not be identical but protection must be equivalent), and remains liable to the controller for the sub-processor's compliance. — [ICO contracts guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/guide-to-accountability-and-governance/contracts/); [RPC summary of ICO guidance](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/data-protection/ico-guidance-on-contracts-and-liabilities-between-controllers-and-processors/)
- Practitioner checklist for DPAs (instructions, confidentiality, security, sub-processor authorisation, assistance with rights requests, deletion/return at termination, audit rights) — [Harper James: UK GDPR data processing agreements](https://harperjames.co.uk/data-agreements.html); [Harper James: processor responsibilities](https://harperjames.co.uk/article/data-processor-responsibilities-uk-gdpr/)
- The ICO audit toolkit suggests contracts are signed and dated by both parties. — [ICO accountability toolkit: contracts and data sharing](https://cy.ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-and-services/audits/data-protection-audit-framework/toolkits/accountability/contracts-and-data-sharing)
- The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (DUAA) amended UK GDPR, DPA 2018 and PECR; main data protection/PECR provisions commenced 5 February 2026 (Commencement Regulations, cited as SI 2026/82 by most sources), with the complaints-procedure provisions from 19 June 2026. — [Clifford Chance, Feb 2026](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2026/02/key-aspects-of-the-data--use-and-access--act-take-effect.html); [HSF Kramer 2026](https://www.hsfkramer.com/ja/notes/data/2026-posts/uk-data-protection-and-privacy-reform-goes-live-what-you-need-to-know); [Bratby Law](https://bratby.law/data-use-and-access-act-2025-commencement/) (Bratby names it Commencement No. 5 — minor conflict on numbering)
- From 19 June 2026, controllers must have a process for handling data protection complaints (DUAA). — [Didomi DUAA summary](https://didomi.io/blog/data-use-and-access-act-duaa-2025); [Stevens & Bolton](https://www.stevens-bolton.com/insights/102mgxs/the-data-use-and-access-act-2025-key-reforms-in-force/)

### Inferences
- Seller checklist: (a) publish a standard DPA (Article 28-compliant) as a schedule to the Terms of Service, accepted on sign-up by the club's authorised officer; (b) include a sub-processor list (GitHub/Microsoft, OneSignal, analytics, payment provider, email) with "general authorisation + notice of changes + right to object" mechanism; (c) keep a record of processing activities as processor; (d) breach-notification clause committing to tell the club "without undue delay" so the club can meet the 72-hour ICO deadline; (e) export/delete at end of season or contract.
- Club checklist: lawful basis for each data type (contract/legitimate interests for squad admin; consent for photos/publishing names of under-18s per FA guidance); privacy notice to members/parents naming the app supplier; retention rules; a named person (often club secretary/welfare officer) handling subject access requests; a complaints route (post-June 2026).
- "Fines" data (club internal fines) is personal data about conduct; keep it visible only to the player/admins, not public, to minimise reputational harm and fairness complaints.
- Advantage: a short plain-English "Data protection pack" for club committees (template privacy notice, consent wording, DPA, sub-processor list) would remove a key purchasing objection.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch full ICO text; the Article 28 terms above are from ICO page extracts and well-established Article 28(3) content.
- No specific ICO guidance found on grassroots sports club apps.

## 2. ICO data protection fee

### Takeaway
Fees rose on 17 February 2025: Tier 1 £52 (was £40), Tier 2 £78, Tier 3 £3,763, £5 off by direct debit. Only controllers pay; a pure processor does not. A sole trader app seller is likely a controller for some data (customers' contact/billing data, own analytics, marketing), so will likely need to pay Tier 1 unless every controller activity falls in an exemption.

### Cited Findings
- Data Protection (Charges and Information) (Amendment) Regulations 2025 laid 27 January 2025; new fees effective 17 February 2025: Tier 1 £52, Tier 2 £78, Tier 3 £3,763 (29.8% rise; consultation had proposed 37.2%). — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/02/06/fee-payable-to-the-ico-to-increase-in-february-102jzb9); [ICO: changes to the data protection fee](https://cy.ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/changes-to-the-data-protection-fee); [Gov.uk consultation outcome](https://gov.uk/government/consultations/data-protection-fee-regime-proposed-changes/outcome/data-protection-fee-regime-government-response); conflicting Tier 3 figure £3,726 in [Mercator](https://www.mercator.net/our-thinking/latest-news/united-kingdom-changes-to-uk-data-protection-fee/) — majority incl. ICO table say £3,763.
- Tier 1 (micro organisations) = turnover under £632,000 or no more than 10 staff; £5 direct debit discount. — [Acuity Law](https://acuitylaw.com/rise-in-ico-fees-data-protection-fees-february/); [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/02/06/fee-payable-to-the-ico-to-increase-in-february-102jzb9)
- A Bar Council document titled "Data Protection Fees payable to the ICO July 2026" exists, indicating the fee remains current in 2026 (contents not checked). — [Bar Council Ethics](https://www.barcouncilethics.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Data-Protection-Fees-payable-to-the-ICO-July-2026.pdf)
- Only controllers pay the fee; "You do not need to pay the fee if you are only a 'processor'". Exemptions exist for controllers who process only for certain purposes (e.g. staff administration, accounts and records, own advertising/marketing/PR, not-for-profit purposes). Exempt organisations still have to comply with all other data protection duties. — [ICO exemptions](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/data-protection-fee/exemptions); [Pinsent Masons Out-Law](https://pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/ico-issues-guidance-on-new-data-protection-fee); [ACCA](https://www.accaglobal.com/uk/en/technical-activities/technical-resources-search/2018/april/ico-gdpr-fees.html); [Gov.uk response to ICO charge exemption consultation](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5be3222aed915d6a0a124d08/Government_response_to_the_ICO_Charge_Exemption_Consultation_L.pdf)
- One law firm states processors must pay too — contradicts ICO-derived guidance; treat as wrong. — [Briffa](https://www.briffa.com/blog/ico-fees-and-gdpr-fines-for-small-businesses-and-sole-traders/)

### Inferences
- Seller: run the ICO self-assessment. If seller only processes club data on instructions and keeps its own customer records purely for "accounts and records/own marketing", it may be exempt; but running visitor analytics for its own product purposes, or holding any other personal data as controller, likely triggers Tier 1 (£52, or £47 by direct debit). Paying £52 is cheap and is a selling point ("ICO registered" — clubs and welfare officers recognise it).
- Clubs: many unincorporated grassroots clubs may fall within the not-for-profit exemption (processing only for membership/club purposes); clubs should do the ICO self-assessment themselves.

### Gaps
- Could not read the live ICO fee page or the current penalty amounts for non-payment (historically up to £4,350); not verified, so not relied on.
- Exact current wording of the not-for-profit exemption not retrieved.

## 3. Children's data: UK GDPR, age 13, and the ICO Children's Code

### Takeaway
UK age of digital consent for information society services (ISS) is 13 (DPA 2018 s.9 modifying UK GDPR Art 8), but this only matters where consent is the lawful basis and the service is offered directly to the child. The Children's Code (Age Appropriate Design Code) applies to ISS "likely to be accessed" by under-18s, regardless of target audience; a youth club app with junior player logins or public pages read by juniors is probably in scope.

### Cited Findings
- DPA 2018 s.9 substitutes 13 for 16 in UK GDPR Art 8(1) and excludes preventive/counselling services from "information society services". — [legislation.gov.uk DPA 2018 s.9](https://legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2018/12/section/9/enacted); [ICO: rules about an ISS and consent](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/children-and-the-uk-gdpr/what-are-the-rules-about-an-iss-and-consent)
- Below 13, consent must be given/authorised by a person with parental responsibility; age verification is implied (reasonable efforts) though not explicit. — [ICO ISS and consent](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/children-and-the-uk-gdpr/what-are-the-rules-about-an-iss-and-consent); [Fieldfisher Art 8 commentary](https://ukgdpr.fieldfisher.com/chapter-2/article-8-gdpr/)
- A 2026 Act, s.72, inserts a new Art 8ZA on age verification and gives power to alter the age threshold by regulations; commencement status unconfirmed. — [legislation.gov.uk ukpga/2026/21 s.72](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/21/section/72/data.html)
- Children's Code applies to ISS likely to be accessed by children in the UK, including services not aimed at children; "child" = under 18; ISS = service normally provided for remuneration, at a distance, by electronic means, at individual request. — [ICO: likely to be accessed by children](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/likely-to-be-accessed-by-children/); [ICO: age appropriate design code](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/guide-to-data-protection/ico-codes-of-practice/age-appropriate-design-code); [Shepherd and Wedderburn](https://shepwedd.com/knowledge/childrens-code/)
- Commentary that the Code can apply even to non-profit services, as the ICO treats them as "normally provided for remuneration". — [Stevens & Bolton on edtech](https://viewpoints.stevens-bolton.com/post/102ktzs/ico-the-childrens-code-and-education-technologies)
- Code overview and duties (best interests, DPIA, high privacy by default, data minimisation, no nudge techniques, geolocation off by default, etc.). — [Harper James: Children's Code](https://harperjames.co.uk/article/data-protection-and-the-icos-childrens-code/); [Ashfords](https://ashfords.co.uk/insights/articles/the-ico-s-children-s-code); [ICO Annex B age and developmental stages](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/guide-to-data-protection/ico-codes-of-practice/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/annex-b-age-and-developmental-stages)

### Inferences
- Simplest compliant design: do not rely on children's consent at all. Club admin (adults) enters data under contract/legitimate interests; parents give FA-form consent for photos/name publication. Junior logins, if any, should be optional, read-only, with high-privacy defaults.
- Seller as designer/provider of the service should carry out (or provide a template for) a DPIA covering under-18s; the Children's Code expects one. Apply: no public full names + photos of juniors by default; public pages show first name/initial only or nothing for youth teams; no geolocation; no profiling or behavioural ads; analytics aggregate only.
- Who is the "provider" of the ISS for Code purposes (club or seller) is not settled for white-label club apps; prudent approach is for the seller to build the Code standards in by default — a strong competitive advantage when selling to welfare officers.

### Gaps
- No source specifically on whether a grassroots club's member app is an "ISS normally provided for remuneration"; ICO guidance pages could not be fetched in full.
- Commencement of the 2026 Act s.72 not confirmed.

## 4. FA safeguarding: photos, names, websites/social media, communicating with under-18s, England Football Accreditation

### Takeaway
FA Guidance Notes 6.1 (Running websites and social media), 6.2 (Digital communications and children under 18s), 8.2 (Club Annual Membership Information and Consent Form) and 8.3 (Photographing and filming children) set the rules. Core points: publish photos only with parental consent via the FA form; do not publish names with photos without the relevant permission; no children's contact details in public areas; appoint an adult to monitor club digital channels; communications for under-18 players go via parents/carers, group not 1:1, no texting as primary method; observable/interruptible online contact.

### Cited Findings
- Guidance Note 8.3 "Photographing and Filming Children" (current version on thefa.com dated 21-10-2025 folder; v1.3 2025): FA supports appropriate photos/filming but recognises risks on websites, social media, posters and press; consent collected at start of season via the FA Club Annual Membership Information and Consent Form (Guidance Note 8.2); do not publish photographs or film with the name(s) of individuals featured unless you have the relevant permission; take care with "tagging"; no alcohol/gambling advertising in images of children; keep images of children under care proceedings/with legal restrictions out of the public domain (contact Safeguarding@TheFA.com); inform parents in advance when a professional photographer attends; share the FA guidance with all members. — [FA GN 8.3 (thefa.com)](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/thefacom-new/files/rules-and-regulations/safeguarding/21-10-2025/83-photography-and-filming-children.ashx); [Westmorland FA copy](https://thefa.com/-/media/cfa/westmorlandfa/files/articles/2020/photos/8-3-photographing-and-filming-children-colour-version-(1).ashx?la=en); [East Riding FA 2024](https://eastridingfa.com/news/2024/nov/20/filming-and-photography-2024)
- Guidance Note 6.1 "Running websites and social media platforms" (v1.2, 2025): appoint an adult to monitor the site and make members aware who it is; apply FA photography guidance; use FA Respect Codes of Conduct and Relationships of Trust statement as benchmark; do not host children's/young people's details in public areas where they can be seen or used to contact them; never host pictures of individuals without the permission of players/carers; older FA "Running a website" guidance also advises against identifying details such as school/class in player profiles. — [FA GN 6.1](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/thefacom-new/files/rules-and-regulations/safeguarding/21-10-2025/61-running-websites-and-social-media-platforms.ashx); [FA Running a website do's and don'ts](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/cfa/global/files/safeguarding/guidance-for-clubs-and-leagues---running-a-website.ashx); [Lincolnshire FA best practice](https://www.lincolnshirefa.com/news/2020/sep/21/best-practice-for-running-websites-and-social-media-channels); [FA digital platforms best practice](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/cfa/global/files/safeguarding/digital-platforms---best-practice-guidance.ashx)
- Guidance Note 6.2 "Digital communications and children (under 18s)" (latest version in a thefa.com folder dated 11-11-2026; earlier 21-10-2025): communications for under-18 players should go to parents/carers; online sessions with under-18s must be observable and interruptible, with a second official where possible; texting not to be used as the primary method of communication between adults and children; coaches should not accept under-18 players/referees as social media friends; for 16–17s in positions of responsibility (e.g. young referees/coaches) obtain signed parental consent for email/social contact, keep to the professional role, copy in parent/Welfare Officer; report inappropriate communication immediately to the Welfare Officer, who escalates to the County FA Designated Safeguarding Officer. — [FA GN 6.2 (2026)](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/thefacom-new/files/rules-and-regulations/safeguarding/section-6/11-11-2026/62-digital-communications-children-under-18s.ashx); [FA GN 6.2 (2025)](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/thefacom-new/files/rules-and-regulations/safeguarding/21-10-2025/62-digital-communications-and-children-u18s.ashx); [FA communicating responsibly with under 18s](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/cfa/global/files/safeguarding/guidance-for-leagues-and-clubs---communicating-responsibly-with-under-18s.ashx); [Cheshire FA](https://www.cheshirefa.com/news/2020/may/12/online-safeguarding-guidance-for-communicating-with-u18s); [JPL copy of policy](https://www.juniorpremierleague.com/england/policies/digital-communications-and-children-under-18s/)
- Clubs with under-18s in open-age (adult) teams must adopt the Safeguarding Children Policy. — [FA GN 10.5 best practice in open-age football](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/thefacom-new/files/rules-and-regulations/safeguarding/section-10/09-10-2026/105-best-practice-in-openage-adult-football.ashx)
- FA safeguarding risk-assessment template lists "use of photographs and filming" as an area to assess. — [FA GN 1.9 risk assessment template](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/thefacom-new/files/rules-and-regulations/safeguarding/section-1/02-11-2026/19-safeguarding-risk-assessment-guidance-template.ashx)
- England Football Accreditation replaced Charter Standard (clubs that held Charter Standard were automatically awarded it); renewal uses criteria checks incl. in-date DBS for all youth officials/coaches; safeguarding courses (Safeguarding Children, Safeguarding for Committee Members, Welfare Officer course); Codes of Conduct advisable on club website/social media. — [Staffordshire FA accreditation renewal guidance](https://thefa.com/-/media/cfa/staffordshirefa/files/accreditation/club_accreditation_renewal_guidance.ashx?la=en); [London FA youth clubs](https://www.londonfa.com/leagues-and-clubs/england-football-accreditation/youth-clubs); [Suffolk FA welfare officer pack 2023-24](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/cfa/suffolkfa/files/club-welfare-officer-welcome-pack-2023-24.ashx); [FA Grassroots Technology accreditation checklist](https://grassrootstechnology.thefa.com/support/solutions/articles/48001201397-accreditation-checklist-club)

### Inferences
- App design must-haves for youth clubs: per-player "photo consent" and "name publication consent" flags (mirroring the FA 8.2 form) that the app enforces on public pages; no public display of juniors' contact details, DOB, school; public youth squad pages show first name + initial or nothing; no 1:1 messaging between adults and under-18s — messages/push go to parent/guardian accounts or team groups; admin audit log; club welfare officer role with visibility of comms.
- Push notifications to youth players' own devices could conflict with GN 6.2's "via parents/carers" principle; default youth notifications to parent subscribers.
- Feature advantage: exportable consent register + "Codes of Conduct" page supports England Football Accreditation evidence.
- No evidence that England Football Accreditation imposes specific technical requirements on third-party club apps; the requirements bite on the club's conduct and policies.

## 5. PECR and cookies: push notifications and analytics (GoatCounter)

### Takeaway
Since 5 February 2026, DUAA amendments to PECR exempt certain storage/access from consent, including purely statistical analytics used to improve a service (with information and a simple opt-out), plus security, functionality and similar categories. GoatCounter stores nothing on the device, so PECR storage/access consent is unlikely to be triggered at all; web push requires the browser's own opt-in and is functionally "requested by the user", but marketing pushes would need marketing consent.

### Cited Findings
- DUAA PECR changes effective 5 February 2026 include an exemption for cookies that solely collect information for statistical purposes about use of the service; exemption is narrow (aggregate insights to improve the site/service; not if data feeds advertising). Other exempt categories: security, functionality/user preferences, software updates (some sources add interface customisation). Users must still be informed and higher-risk (ads/social) cookies still need consent. — [Clifford Chance Feb 2026](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2026/02/key-aspects-of-the-data--use-and-access--act-take-effect.html); [Harper James](https://harperjames.co.uk/news/what-the-data-use-and-access-act-means-for-marketers-and-website-cookies/); [Didomi](https://didomi.io/blog/data-use-and-access-act-duaa-2025); [Secure Privacy](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/uk-data-use-and-access-act-2025-pecr-cookie-consent-changes-and-how-to-comply)
- DUAA raised PECR penalties substantially (aligned with UK GDPR levels). — [Bratby Law](https://bratby.law/data-use-and-access-act-2025-commencement/); [Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/the-uk-s-data-protection-reforms-finally-arrive-what-you-should-know-about-the-data-use-and-access-act)
- PECR storage and access rules are not limited to websites or browsers; cover any storage/access technology; consent to UK GDPR standard; legitimate interests cannot replace PECR consent. — [ICO: What are the PECR rules? (storage and access technologies)](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/what-are-the-pecr-rules/); [ICO: managing consent in practice](https://cy.ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/how-do-we-manage-consent-in-practice/)
- Electronic direct marketing rules (reg 22) apply to promotional messages; pure service messages are not direct marketing. — [ICO electronic and telephone marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing); [Sprintlaw PECR](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/pecr-regulations-uk-e-privacy-rules-for-marketing-cookies-and-comms/)
- GoatCounter: no cookies, localStorage or other browser storage; no unique identifiers; its own GDPR page says it "probably" does not need a consent notice (legitimate interest; no PII) while noting legal uncertainty. — [GoatCounter GDPR](https://www.goatcounter.com/gdpr); [GoatCounter privacy](https://goatcounter.com/privacy); [TechGDPR directory](https://techgdpr.com/privacy-tech-directory/goatcounter/)

### Inferences
- GoatCounter: no cookie banner needed for PECR; still list it in the privacy notice (it processes IP/user-agent transiently server-side) and rely on legitimate interests. Note GoatCounter is hosted outside the UK (EU-based operator) — mention in sub-processor list.
- Web push (OneSignal or native Push API): the subscription stores an identifier on the device; the browser permission prompt plus an in-app explanation (what you'll get, how to stop) is a clear positive action — treat as consent and log it. Keep notifications to club/match service messages; any promotional push (sponsor offers, seller marketing) needs separate marketing consent. For under-18s, route to parents (see section 4).
- Functional storage (login session, preferences) falls within strictly necessary/functionality exemptions.

### Gaps
- No ICO guidance found specific to web push / service workers.
- Exact DUAA PECR maximum fine figure not verified here.

## 6. Hosting location, sub-processors and international transfers (GitHub Pages, OneSignal)

### Takeaway
Both GitHub (Microsoft) and OneSignal are US-based; using them means restricted transfers out of the UK, which need a mechanism (UK Extension to EU-US Data Privacy Framework "data bridge" if certified, or IDTA / UK Addendum to EU SCCs) and must appear in the sub-processor list. GitHub Pages is static hosting and logs visitor IPs for security; club personal data stored in a public GitHub repo/Pages site would be publicly accessible — a significant design risk.

### Cited Findings
- OneSignal DPA: for UK GDPR transfers it relies on EU SCCs plus the ICO International Data Transfer Addendum ("UK SCCs"); publishes a sub-processor list (Annex III) and flows down obligations. — [OneSignal DPA](https://onesignal.com/dpa); [OneSignal EU DPA PDF](https://media-cms.onesignal.com/cms/Files/OneSignal_EU_DPA.pdf)
- Current OneSignal DPF/UK Extension certification not confirmed in search results. — [OneSignal GDPR blog (Privacy Shield era)](https://onesignal.com/blog/how-onesignal-meets-gdpr-compliance-measures/)
- GitHub: a Data Protection Agreement governs GitHub's relationship with data controllers; GitHub privacy statement covers international transfers/SCCs and DPF principles; GitHub logs visitor IP addresses for Pages for security purposes and advises Pages owners to publish their own privacy notice (wording via secondary sources). — [GitHub General Privacy Statement](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement); [GitHub Pages docs](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/what-is-github-pages); [GitHub blog: revised DPA with new SCCs](https://github.blog/news-insights/policy-news-and-insights/revised-enterprise-dpa-new-standard-contractual-clauses); [ConductAtlas summary of GitHub privacy statement transfers](https://conductatlas.com/platform/github/github-privacy-statement/international-data-transfers-and-standard-contractual-clauses/)

### Inferences
- If player data (names, stats, fines) is committed as JSON into a GitHub repo served by Pages, it is effectively published to the internet (and in git history). For youth clubs this likely conflicts with FA GN 6.1/8.3 and data minimisation. Use an access-controlled backend (e.g. UK/EU-region database) for anything non-public; keep Pages for the static app shell only. Strong advantage: "UK/EU-hosted data" claim.
- Seller must: list GitHub, OneSignal, GoatCounter, any DB/email/payment providers as sub-processors; keep copies of their DPAs and transfer mechanisms; do a short transfer risk assessment; tell clubs in the DPA.
- OneSignal: minimise data sent (no names in push payloads or tags for juniors); use its UK/EU-transfer DPA.

### Gaps
- Could not verify GitHub's and OneSignal's current DPF / UK Extension listings (check dataprivacyframework.gov list).
- GitHub's current Pages-specific logging wording comes from secondary sources.

## 7. Commercial basics: terms, liability, insurance, contracting with unincorporated clubs, VAT

### Takeaway
Most grassroots clubs are unincorporated associations with no separate legal personality, so the contract is in practice with the committee members who sign/authorise it — make the terms simple, low-value, rolling/annual, and name the signing officer. VAT registration is compulsory only once taxable turnover exceeds £90,000 in a rolling 12 months (from 1 April 2024; deregistration £88,000).

### Cited Findings
- Unincorporated club: no separate legal identity; committee members who sign (and those who authorise) contracts are personally bound/liable; ordinary members' liability generally limited to subscriptions; mitigations include incorporation, insurance and constitution limits. — [Sport Wales: service level agreements](https://sport.wales/grants-and-funding/club-support/managing-your-sports-club/planning/service-level-agreements); [FA: A guide to club structures (Oct 2020)](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/cfa/amateurfa/files/a-guide-to-club-structures-october-2020.ashx); [LTA template constitution guidance 2024](https://www.lta.org.uk/491a81/siteassets/roles/venues/file/guidance-on-template-constitution-of-unincorporated-association-casc-2024.pdf); [British Taekwondo: unincorporated organisations](https://www.britishtaekwondo.org.uk/my-club/club-business-unit/05-legal-setup-guidance/unincorporated-organisations/)
- VAT registration threshold rose from £85,000 to £90,000 and deregistration from £83,000 to £88,000 on 1 April 2024 (SI 2024/307 amending VATA 1994 Sch 1). — [GOV.UK: Increasing the VAT registration threshold](https://gov.uk/government/publications/vat-increasing-the-registration-and-deregistration-thresholds/increasing-the-vat-registration-threshold); [GOV.UK press release 1 April 2024](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/boost-for-small-business-as-vat-threshold-raised-and-rates-frozen); [legislation.gov.uk SI 2024/307](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/307/made)
- Third-party 2026 guides still show £90,000 as current. — [Tide](https://www.tide.co/blog/tax/vat-threshold/); [Lookuptax](https://lookuptax.com/tax-changes/united-kingdom/vat-registration-threshold-90k-2024)
- Clubs/not-for-profits commonly consider D&O / civil liability insurance. — [DAC Beachcroft on D&O for clubs](https://www.dacbeachcroft.com/fr/What-we-think/Members-behaving-badly-the-importance-of-DO-insurance-for-Clubs-and-NotforProfit-organisations)

### Inferences
- Terms of Service should cover: who the customer is (the club acting by its named officer, who confirms authority); fees, renewal, cancellation; service description and availability (no SLA guarantee for low price); acceptable use (club responsible for content/consents it uploads — esp. photos/names of juniors); IP licence; data protection schedule (DPA); liability cap (e.g. fees paid in prior 12 months) with carve-outs that cannot be excluded (death/personal injury from negligence, fraud) — subject to the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 reasonableness test for business customers; governing law England & Wales; data export/deletion on termination.
- Insurance (advantage, not legal requirement for a sole trader): professional indemnity / tech E&O covering data breach and negligent service; cyber insurance; public liability unlikely to matter for a pure software supplier.
- Below £90,000 turnover, no VAT registration needed; pricing can be VAT-free — an advantage for clubs that are not VAT-registered (most grassroots clubs). Register as self-employed for income tax (sole trader).

## 8. Payments: subs/fines via Stripe — FCA / Payment Services Regulations

### Takeaway
Under the Payment Services Regulations 2017, anyone providing payment services as a business needs FCA authorisation/registration unless excluded. A seller avoids this by never holding or controlling club money: each club has its own Stripe account (e.g. Stripe Connect Standard/direct charges, or Stripe Payment Links owned by the club) so funds go straight from payer to club via Stripe, which is the authorised payment institution. The commercial agent exclusion is unlikely to fit a subs/fines collection platform.

### Cited Findings
- PSRs 2017: providing payment services as a regular occupation/business requires FCA authorisation or registration. — [FCA: Commercial agent exclusion](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/commercial-agent-exclusion-cae)
- Commercial agent exclusion (Sch 1 Pt 2 para (b)): transactions through an agent authorised to negotiate or conclude the sale/purchase for payer or payee; platforms must consider PERG 15 Annex 3 para (b); escrow-type and donation-passing platforms generally do not qualify. — [FCA CAE](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/commercial-agent-exclusion-cae); [FCA PERG 15.5](https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/PERG/15/5.html); [Osborne Clarke on platforms and marketplaces](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/evolution-retail-platforms-and-marketplaces-risks-additional-regulatory-consequences)
- Technical service provider exclusion (PERG 15 Annex 3 para (j)) covers providers that support payment services without ever possessing the funds. — [FCA PERG 15 Annex 3](https://www.Handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/PERG/15/Annex3.pdf)

### Inferences
- Safe structure: club is the merchant of record; seller's app only creates checkout sessions / links on the club's Stripe account; seller never receives club funds into its own account. Seller then sits within the technical service provider exclusion. If seller wants a platform fee, use Stripe Connect application fees on direct charges (Stripe remains the regulated party) — confirm with Stripe's platform terms.
- Avoid: collecting subs into the seller's own Stripe/bank account and paying clubs out later — that looks like money remittance/acquiring and could need FCA authorisation.
- Card data never touches the seller (Stripe Checkout/Elements) — keeps PCI DSS scope to SAQ A.
- Fines collected from players: club-internal; the app should present them as club charges, with the club's own policy.

### Gaps
- No source found applying PSRs analysis specifically to Stripe Connect account types; Stripe's own platform/Connect UK regulatory documentation was not retrieved.
- PCI DSS SAQ A point is from general knowledge, not sourced here.

# Price the matchday app as managed content

A per-club matchday app like the Ludham FC one should sell for **about £90-£120 a season self-serve and £25-£35 a month (£300-£420 a year) as a done-for-you service**, plus a one-off setup fee. The ceiling is set by Pitchero, the default "club website + app", at **£38-£99 a month (£456-£1,188 a year)** ([Pitchero help centre](https://help.pitchero.com/knowledge/pitchero-club-website/packages/manage-package)). The floor is set by free tools: Spond, the FA's Matchday app and WhatsApp. The managed service has no club-app competitor. The nearest comparisons are graphics-only services charging **£20-£180 a month** ([Footyfits](https://footyfits.co.uk/products/club-social-media-content-package); [TRYMF](https://trymf.com/)). England has **more than 124,500 affiliated teams** ([FA, Sep 2026](https://www.thefa.com/news/2026/sep/28/a-thriving-grassroots-game-update-20262809)) in an estimated 20,000-30,000 clubs. That puts the theoretical club-level market at a few million pounds a year. A one-person business will hit a capacity limit long before it hits the market limit. Compliance is cheap but must be done properly: an Article 28 data processing agreement, an ICO fee of £52, FA safeguarding defaults for any youth club, and a payments design that never touches club money. The existing encryption is a real strength, though passwords are shared and the encrypted files can be downloaded. The defensible moat is not any single feature. It is the input pipeline (one email in, everything out), the club history archive, a money ledger that understands fines, and sponsor reports that pay for the app. Competitors can copy features in weeks. They cannot easily copy those.

## Competitors split into free organisers, £300-£1,200 club platforms and graphics services

The English grassroots market has three layers, and the Ludham-style app sits across all three without matching any of them.

**Free organisers** own availability and payments. **Spond** is free and ad-free. It earns money mainly on payments at **2.5% + 20p** per transaction ([Spond help: transaction fees](https://help.spond.com/club/en/articles/58192-what-are-the-transaction-costs-for-payments-made-in-spond-club)) and on its Superdraw fundraiser, where it keeps 35% ([Spond fundraising terms](https://www.spond.com/en-us/fundraising-terms-stb/)). It claims **over 1.5 million UK users** ([Spond](https://www.spond.com/news-and-blog/how-to-manage-grassroots-football-clubs/)), but it has no player stats or fines, and its match reports are limited to a score and a Player of the Match vote ([TeamStats vs Spond](https://www.teamstats.net/football-coaching/apps/teamstats-vs-spond)). The **FA Matchday app** is free and pulls fixtures from Full-Time and squads from the Whole Game System. It handles availability, line-ups and result submission to the league ([England Football](https://www.englandfootball.com/participate/leagues-and-clubs/helpful-apps-and-websites/matchday)). It produces no fan-facing content at all: no graphics, no narrative reports, no supporter view. TeamApp and Heja are free and ad-funded. **Teamer closed on 5 October 2026**, and its owner, Pitchero Group, is steering users to Pitchero ([Teamer Shutdown FAQs](https://help.teamer.net/knowledge/teamer-shutdown-faqs)). That leaves a pool of displaced teams this season.

**Paid club platforms** set the price anchors for an ad-free, club-branded product. Pitchero's Free tier covers one team and carries third-party ads. Standard is **£38 a month** and Pro is **£99 a month**, with extra teams at £2.50 a month ([Pitchero pricing](https://www.pitchero.com/pricing)). Pitchero claims over 10,000 clubs ([Pitchero About](https://pitchero.com/about?amp=)), but its UK Trustpilot score is 2.5/5, from only eight reviews ([Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/www.pitchero.com)). ClubBuzz Standard costs **£299 a year** ([ClubBuzz pricing](https://www.clubbuzz.co.uk/pricing)). SportMember PRO costs £0.16 per user per month, with a **£22 a month minimum** ex VAT ([SportMember](https://www.sportmember.co.uk/en/what-does-it-cost/clubsubscriptions)). **TeamStats** is the closest feature rival. It has player stats, automatic fines from match data and AI "newspaper-style" match reports, at **$7-$14 per team per month** ([TeamStats pricing](https://www.teamstats.net/pricing); [App Store UK](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/teamstats-football-team-app/id1275644100)). Spond's club website costs about £19 a month after a six-month trial ([Spond](https://www.spond.com/news-and-blog/club-website-spond/)).

**Graphics and content services** are a separate spend. Terrace is free with a watermark, or **£6 a month** for Plus ([Terrace](https://myterrace.net/pricing/)). Matchday Media charges £15, £30 or £55 a month ([Matchday Media](https://www.matchdaymedia.co.uk/services-4)). Footyfits makes graphics by hand for **£20 setup plus £20 a month** ([Footyfits](https://footyfits.co.uk/products/club-social-media-content-package)). TRYMF runs a managed non-league service at **£600-£1,800 a year** ([TRYMF](https://trymf.com/)). FootBot outputs result graphics and MOTM posts with sponsor logos from scores entered by hand ([FootBot](https://statbot-5o3.pages.dev/social-media-content)).

|---|---|---|---|
| Pitchero Standard / Pro | Website + app, payments, live scores | £38 / £99 per month | Supporter site, push; no confirmed graphics or fines |
| ClubBuzz Standard | Website, no ads | £299/yr | Supporter site only |

Most of the figures above come from search extracts and from comparison pages written by rival vendors, because the vendor pricing pages could not be fetched. Check each one on the live page before quoting it to a club. The pattern holds even so: **no surveyed product bundles stats, fines, match reports, ready-to-paste WhatsApp messages, auto social graphics, a supporter view and sponsor slots in one ad-free, club-themed package**. Only graphics vendors, not app vendors, offer a done-for-you service. A club that wants what Ludham has would today pay for Pitchero or ClubBuzz, plus TeamStats, plus a graphics tool, and still do all the typing itself.

## Recommended prices: £90 a season self-serve, £300-£420 managed, £600 premium

Club budgets set the ceiling. Grassroots clubs have little money and costs are rising: **99% of clubs report higher costs and 57% say funding is insufficient** ([FORZA 2026](https://www.forza.com/uk/football/grassroots-report)). One guide puts a small club's season at about **£8,000**, of which pitch hire is £1,800-£3,600 ([Avec Sport](https://avecsport.com/blog/what-are-the-costs-of-running-a-football-club)). Referees cost £40-£50 per adult game ([Avec Sport](https://avecsport.com/blog/what-are-the-costs-of-running-a-football-club)). County FA affiliation costs only about £30-£38 per adult team, plus £70 per club for public liability cover ([Lincolnshire FA](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/cfa/lincolnshirefa/files/affiliation-pack-2025.ashx?la=en); [Hertfordshire FA](https://www.hertfordshirefa.com/news/2024/may/21/club-affiliation-2024-25)). Clubs do pay for digital tools they value. Veo's grassroots Starter plan has cost **£360-£499 a year** on top of a £999 camera ([East Riding FA](https://www.eastridingfa.com/clubs-and-facilities/veo); [Veo UK shop](https://uk.shop.veo.co/)).

Sponsorship is the strongest pricing lever. **71-74% of clubs rely on sponsorship as a main income source** ([FORZA 2026](https://www.forza.com/uk/football/grassroots-report); [FORZA 2024](https://www.networldsports.co.uk/football/grassroots-report-2024)). Small packages are cheap and common: a match-ball sponsor at about £100 ([TeamStats](https://www.teamstats.net/blog/using-local-business-partnerships-to-fund-your-team)), player sponsorship at £200 a season ([Great Yarmouth Town FC](https://gytfc.co.uk/?p=4286)), kit at £250-£2,000 ([ClubHub UK](https://clubhubuk.co.uk/why-so-many-grassroots-kids-clubs-still-cant-afford-matching-kit/)). Set the managed price at **roughly one small sponsor package**. The pitch then becomes "the app gives you sponsor slots you can sell, so it pays for itself", rather than another cost line.

| Tier | Who it is for | Recommended price | Anchors it sits against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Self-serve Club** | Single-team Saturday/Sunday clubs that will type their own updates | **£9/month or £90/season**, +£3/month per extra team | Below ClubBuzz £299/yr and Pitchero Standard £456/yr; in line with TeamStats ($7-$14/month); above Terrace £6/month |
| **Matchday Managed** (done-for-you, update by email) | Clubs with one overloaded volunteer | **£29/month or £300/season**, plus **£75 setup** | Above Footyfits (£20 + £20/month, graphics only); well below TRYMF (£60-£180/month); about the price of one or two £200 player sponsorships |
| **Club Premium** | Multi-team youth clubs, Step 5-6 non-league | **£50-£60/month (about £600/year)**, including history back-fill and a sponsor report | Still below Pitchero Pro (£1,188/yr); on a par with Veo Starter |
| **History back-fill** (one-off) | Any tier | **£150-£300** | No competitor offers it |
| **League deal** | A whole district or Sunday league | About **£60-£75 per club per season**, minimum 8-10 clubs, plus a league sponsor slot | No sourced comparison; Pitchero league pricing not found |

Several details support these numbers. Below **£90,000** taxable turnover there is no VAT to register for ([GOV.UK](https://gov.uk/government/publications/vat-increasing-the-registration-and-deregistration-thresholds/increasing-the-vat-registration-threshold)), so these prices are final to clubs. Most grassroots clubs cannot reclaim VAT, so that is a real advantage over vendors who quote "ex VAT". Seasonal billing (August to May) matches how clubs budget subs ([Sportplan](https://www.sportplan.net/drills/Football/guides/football-subs-match-fees.jsp)). A founding-club price for teams displaced by Teamer's closure, for example the first season at the self-serve rate for the managed service, is a cheap way to win early references.

If a payments feature is added later, take a **small platform fee on the club's own Stripe account** rather than a subscription increase. Stripe charges 1.5% + 20p on standard UK cards ([Stripe](https://support.stripe.com/questions/pricing-updates-for-businesses-in-the-united-kingdom)). A 0.5% platform fee on top still undercuts Spond's 2.5% + 20p. The fixed 20p dominates small payments: a £5 match fee costs about 33p through Spond and 28p through raw Stripe. A monthly "settle your balance" payment beats per-match charging for both club and player.

The pricing has weak points, and they should be stated. No survey shows what grassroots clubs spend on technology. No evidence shows how many clubs pay for Pitchero rather than use its free tier. Many small adult clubs will compare any price with "free plus WhatsApp". The self-serve tier will meet more price resistance than the managed tier, because the managed tier sells saved volunteer hours, not software.

## The market is large; one operator's hours are the limit

The FA counts **124,500+ affiliated teams and 1.6 million affiliated players**. Team numbers are up 10% in two seasons, and female teams grew fastest, up 14.3% between 2023 and 2025 ([FA, Sep 2026](https://www.thefa.com/news/2026/sep/28/a-thriving-grassroots-game-update-20262809); [FA Annual Report](https://www.thefa.com/news/2026/apr/02/2024-25-fa-annual-report)). There is no current national club count. London FA's ratio of about five teams per club ([London FA AGM 2024](https://thefa.com/-/media/cfa/londonfa/files/agm/2024-agm/lfa-agm-2024---ceo-report.ashx)) gives an estimate of about 25,000 clubs, and about **18,000 clubs are FA-accredited** ([House of Lords Library](https://lordslibrary.parliament.uk/non-league-and-grassroots-football-what-is-the-state-of-play/)). The adult Saturday segment closest to Ludham's profile is the fragmented middle. Only about **660 clubs** sit at National League System Steps 5-6 ([FA NLS Regulations 2026-27](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/files/thefaportal/governance-docs/rules-of-the-association/2026-27/section-21---national-league-system-regulations.ashx)). Most adult clubs play in county and feeder leagues below that. Adult Sunday 11-a-side has been shrinking for years ([Telegraph via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/uk/the-daily-telegraph-sport/20180924/281964608640104)). Youth and girls' football is where team numbers and admin pain are growing.

On rough maths, about 25,000 clubs paying £100-£300 a season is a **£2.5m-£7.5m a year** market at full penetration. Pricing per team instead gives £7.5m-£45m. Realistic penetration of 2-10% implies £0.1m-£4.5m. These are estimates, not sourced totals. The practical limit is different. Ludham's email-to-site automation means a managed club may take only minutes a week, but that is an unmeasured assumption. Even so, a solo operator would need **about 300 managed clubs at £300 to reach the £90,000 VAT threshold**. A realistic first-season target is **20-50 managed clubs in one county** (£6,000-£15,000 a year), plus self-serve clubs on top. Sell geographically, league by league. Each club in the same league makes the others' apps more useful, through opponents, head-to-heads and away grounds, and Norfolk is the obvious first league.

## Compliance: a short checklist, plus honest wording about encryption

**Data protection roles.** For player names, stats, fines and squads, the **club is the controller and the seller is its processor**. That makes a written Article 28 contract mandatory. It must cover processing only on instructions, confidentiality, security, sub-processor rules, help with data subject rights and breaches, deletion or return at the end, and audit information ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/contracts-and-liabilities-between-controllers-and-processors-multi/what-needs-to-be-included-in-the-contract/)). Sub-processors need the club's prior authorisation and flow-down terms ([ICO contracts guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/guide-to-accountability-and-governance/contracts/)). List **GitHub (Pages hosting), OneSignal (push) and GoatCounter (analytics)**, plus any email or payment provider. GitHub and OneSignal are US-based, so the list must name the transfer mechanism. OneSignal's DPA relies on EU SCCs plus the UK Addendum ([OneSignal DPA](https://onesignal.com/dpa)). GitHub has its own DPA, and it logs Pages visitor IPs for security ([GitHub privacy statement](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement)). The seller is also a controller for its own customer and billing data. That probably means paying the **ICO Tier 1 fee of £52 (£47 by direct debit)** ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/02/06/fee-payable-to-the-ico-to-increase-in-february-102jzb9)). Since **19 June 2026**, the Data (Use and Access) Act also requires controllers to have a process for handling data protection complaints ([Stevens & Bolton](https://www.stevens-bolton.com/insights/102mgxs/the-data-use-and-access-act-2025-key-reforms-in-force/)). Bundle these into a plain-English "data protection pack" for club committees: Terms, a DPA schedule, the sub-processor list, a template privacy notice and a template complaints route. It removes a common reason for committees to say no.

**Encryption and its limits.** The club data in the published `data.js` is encrypted with **AES-GCM under PBKDF2-derived keys, with one password per audience**. Hosting on GitHub Pages therefore does not expose plain player data, and that is stronger than many club sites manage. Clubs should still be told the residual risks plainly. First, the encrypted file is **publicly downloadable**, so anyone can try passwords offline as fast as their hardware allows, with no rate limit. A short or guessable password ("ludham2026") can be brute-forced. Second, **passwords get shared**, for example forwarded in WhatsApp groups. There is no per-user access control, no audit of who opened what, and no revocation short of changing the password, re-encrypting and redistributing it. Mitigations are simple. Use long random passphrases, especially for the manager and admin versions. Keep the PBKDF2 iteration count high. Rotate passwords each season and whenever someone leaves. Put fines, contact details and anything about under-18s only in the admin or manager payload, never the supporter one. Check git history for any plaintext data committed before encryption. Word the contract as "encrypted at rest with shared passwords", not "access-controlled". If a club needs named logins or instant revocation, especially a youth club, that needs a real authenticated backend.

**Youth clubs.** The ICO **Children's Code** applies to online services "likely to be accessed" by under-18s ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/likely-to-be-accessed-by-children/)). It expects a DPIA, high-privacy defaults and no nudging. The UK age of digital consent is 13 ([DPA 2018 s.9](https://legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2018/12/section/9/enacted)), but the simpler design is never to rely on children's consent. The FA's guidance notes set practical rules. **GN 8.3**: do not publish photos with names without permission, collected through the FA's annual consent form (GN 8.2) ([FA GN 8.3](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/thefacom-new/files/rules-and-regulations/safeguarding/21-10-2025/83-photography-and-filming-children.ashx)). **GN 6.1**: an adult monitors club channels, and no children's details appear in public areas ([FA GN 6.1](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/thefacom-new/files/rules-and-regulations/safeguarding/21-10-2025/61-running-websites-and-social-media-platforms.ashx)). **GN 6.2**, updated November 2026: communications for under-18s go through parents or carers, as group messages, not one-to-one ([FA GN 6.2](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/thefacom-new/files/rules-and-regulations/safeguarding/section-6/11-11-2026/62-digital-communications-children-under-18s.ashx)). For youth teams, the app should therefore have per-player photo and name consent flags that public pages and graphics respect. It should show first names only or nothing on public youth pages, send push alerts to parent subscribers by default, and give the welfare officer a role that can see all communications.

**Cookies and push.** GoatCounter sets no cookies and uses no device storage ([GoatCounter GDPR](https://www.goatcounter.com/gdpr)), so no cookie banner is needed. Since 5 February 2026 the PECR changes also exempt purely statistical analytics ([Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2026/02/key-aspects-of-the-data--use-and-access--act-take-effect.html)). GoatCounter should still be named in the privacy notice. The browser prompt plus an explanation counts as opt-in for push. Match alerts are service messages. **Sponsor offers sent by push are marketing** and need separate consent ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing)).

**Contracts, payments and FA data.** Most clubs are unincorporated associations, so the committee members who sign are personally bound ([Sport Wales](https://sport.wales/grants-and-funding/club-support/managing-your-sports-club/planning/service-level-agreements)). The terms should name the signing officer, cap liability at fees paid in the last year, and run season to season. Under the Payment Services Regulations, the seller **must never hold club money**. If each club is the merchant on its own Stripe account, the seller stays within the technical service provider exclusion ([FCA PERG 15 Annex 3](https://www.Handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/PERG/15/Annex3.pdf)). Collecting subs into the seller's own account and paying clubs out later would need FCA authorisation. FA Full-Time has **no public API**. The FA removed public code snippets to stop scraping, and its terms prohibit text and data mining ([FA Grassroots Technology](https://grassrootstechnology.thefa.com/support/solutions/articles/48001158072-embedding-league-tables-fixtures-tables-full-time-feeds-); [FA terms](https://www.thefa.com/public/terms)). League tables should therefore come from a club admin's own snippet or the club's own entries, not a scraper. Professional indemnity and cyber insurance are optional, but they reassure welfare officers.

## Moats come from the archive, the ledger and the email pipeline, not from features

Most visible features are already copied across the market. POTM voting, live scores and stats are standard in Pitchero, Mingle Sport, Clava and others ([Pitchero](https://help.pitchero.com/knowledge/club-app/scores-match-events); [Soccer Scene](https://soccerscene.com.au/mingle-sport-transforming-grassroots-football-management/)). AI match reports and auto result cards come from TeamStats, FootballGPT and FootBot ([TeamStats](https://www.teamstats.net/blog/generating-match-reports-automatically); [FootballGPT](https://footballgpt.co/features/voice-match-notes)). What volunteers complain about most is **chasing money and chasing replies**. In a GoCardless study, **44% of grassroots officials said they were considering quitting over the admin of chasing payments** ([FF News](https://ffnews.com/news/gocardless-research-reveals-grassroots-football-crisis-as-44-of-volunteers-consider-quitting-over-admin-burden)). That is a vendor survey, but it matches first-hand accounts of managers "like a loan shark" chasing match fees ([Jim Taylor](https://iamjimtaylor.com/posts/running-a-sunday-league-football-team)). Sponsors increasingly want evidence of results, not a logo on a homepage ([Purple Imp](https://purpleimp.com/stop-selling-logos-start-selling-outcomes-sponsorship-roi-for-clubs/)). No public benchmarks exist for grassroots digital impressions. The table ranks moat features by how hard they are to copy and how directly they cut churn.

| Priority | Feature | Why it is a moat | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Productise "one email or voice note in, everything out"**, with a human check on the managed tier | Ludham already runs this. Competitors generate reports but still need structured input. The human-plus-automation service is hard for self-serve SaaS to copy | Low: already built; needs templating per club |
| 2 | **Club history archive with paid back-fill**: career pages, appearance and goal milestones, records, honours board, head-to-head against each opponent | Builds up every season and is painful to rebuild elsewhere. Lets reports say "first win at X since 2019" instead of generic AI text | Medium; back-fill is chargeable |
| 3 | **Money ledger**: running balances (subs + match fees + fines added automatically), a "pay your balance" link to paste into WhatsApp, an optional "owes over £X" flag on the squad picker | Targets the pain that drives volunteers to quit. Switching means reconciling the books. Club-owned Stripe keeps it outside FCA rules | Medium-high |
| 4 | **Sponsor proof report** each month and season: views (GoatCounter), push sends that named the sponsor, sponsor logos on each auto graphic, UTM link clicks; "POTM sponsored by X" and "matchday sponsor" slots on graphics | Turns the app into sellable inventory, so clubs see it pay for itself and renew. Few competitors measure this | Medium |
| 5 | **Safeguarding by design for youth clubs**: consent flags enforced on pages and graphics, parent-routed push, welfare-officer role, exportable consent register, data protection pack | Welfare officers and County FAs can approve it. Supports England Football Accreditation evidence ([FA accreditation checklist](https://grassrootstechnology.thefa.com/support/solutions/articles/48001201397-accreditation-checklist-club)) | Medium |
| 6 | **League-wide deals plus a shared away-ground directory** (postcode, parking, changing rooms, pitch surface, referee notes) | A local network effect: each extra club improves the others' apps. No incumbent found. County FAs do sign commercial partners ([SNAP + Oxfordshire FA](https://site.snapsponsorship.com/news/snap-sponsorship-and-oxfordshire-fa-partner-to-strengthen-grassroots-football-sustainability)) | Medium |
| 7 | **No-install POTM voting and milestone alerts** shared as WhatsApp links ("2 games from 100 apps") with an auto graphic | Cheap engagement that works with WhatsApp rather than trying to replace it | Low |
| 8 | **Free data export** on request | Reduces fear of lock-in and meets GDPR good practice. The lock-in should come from convenience, not trapped data | Low |
| 9 | Club theming and installable icon | Helps adoption, but anyone can copy it | Already built |

A few small touches make the content feel like the club's own rather than generated. Add a **"manager's comment" line** in the manager's own words on every report; TeamStats itself admits automated reports work best with room for coach observations ([TeamStats](https://www.teamstats.net/blog/generating-match-reports-automatically)). Use real squad photos where consent exists, the club's actual nicknames and in-jokes kept in a per-club style note, and history-based facts in every report. Show sponsor offers as perks members can redeem, such as a discount at the sponsor's pub, so the supporter version has a reason to be opened midweek. In a small community, generic AI prose is easy to spot. The done-for-you human check is part of the product, not overhead.

## Conclusion

The price point and the moat point the same way. Clubs will not pay much for software that competes with Spond and the FA's free tools. They will pay something close to a small sponsorship deal to stop one volunteer doing hours of typing, chasing and posting each week, especially if the app creates the sponsor inventory that covers its own cost. That favours leading with **Matchday Managed at about £300 a season**, sold league by league starting in Norfolk, with self-serve as the cheaper entry tier. The gap is open now: Teamer has just closed, Pitchero is moving users onto pricier plans, and no rival bundles content, stats, fines and supporter engagement.

The biggest risk is not a competitor. It is trust. The encrypted-file model is good enough for an adult Saturday club. It is not a long-term answer for youth clubs that need named logins and instant revocation, or for the money ledger. Those two moat features will eventually require a proper authenticated backend. The legal paperwork (DPA, sub-processor list, ICO fee, safeguarding defaults) should be in place before the first paying club, not after, because a welfare officer's approval is the strongest reference in this market.
